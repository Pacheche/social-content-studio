# Film planning and review

Use this pass for a scripted short, narrative ad, longer chaptered film or music video. It works with the engines and approval mode already selected for the project.

## Pick the depth

- **Compact production:** one production card, key stills and a low-resolution cut. Use one scene system when the film can share a layout and timing model. A half-size draft can expose weak timing and framing. Review phone-sized type and final encoding on the actual deliverable.
- **Staged production:** roadmap, script and on-screen text, style frame with type, animatic, then draft and finish. Use it when story, style, expensive rendering or chapter seams make late revisions costly. Show the artifact that resolves a decision before asking for that decision. Combine gates when the user has authorized working through them.

Add these to the existing spec. Keep a single copy of decisions shared with the OpenMotion spec or project contract.

| Item | Record |
|---|---|
| Purpose | The promised viewer experience for a film, or the actual benefit, proof and available action for an ad |
| Ending | The change, reveal or image that pays off the opening |
| Beat or shot | Time in/out, action the viewer can see, approved words, lead element, transition out and sound cue |
| Look and type | A frame target with real lighting, framing and type roles. Include characters and accents used in the actual copy when checking font coverage |
| Music and voice | Source and rights, intended role, timing grid, and a short audition when the choice is material |
| Finish | Any justified grade, motion blur or texture. Apply after checking its effect on legibility, brand colors and adjacent shots |

## Scene contract for narrated motion

When a piece is driven by voice-over or spoken narration, lock the timing from a timecoded transcript before splitting visual work across layers (type, UI/character, audio, motion):

| Start–End | Spoken line | Visual / layer | Action & emphasis | Sound cue | Review check |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `00:00.0–00:02.8` | Approved opening line | Lead visual or real UI state | One clear focal move on key phrase | Hit or bed entry on downbeat | Readable at phone width; audio clear |

Freeze the scene timings before parallel work starts, scale roles to the project size with one person integrating the scenes from a single timing source, and verify intelligible voice plus cue alignment on the exported file (not just the script).

## Write and build the cut

1. Start with the ending and work back to the question the first beat opens. Compare the obvious approach with a genuinely different visual device and choose based on the brief. Give every chapter a purpose and a handoff to the next. Use narration for what the image cannot convey.
2. Turn the beat table into an animatic using existing stills, temporary narration or sequence placeholders. Review the promise, shot order, reading time, sound entry and exit, and repeated beats before committing to full-resolution work. Keep placeholder assets identified and replace them before delivery.
3. For a reference, measure cuts, holds, pacing and sound. Record observed details, your interpretation, what to carry over and what the user wants changed. Carry over the formal decisions and make story, subjects and assets from the approved material. The [OpenMotion reference route](openmotion.md) adds editable reconstruction and motion measurement when those are part of the request.
4. For a song-led cut, locate the usable excerpt, downbeat, bars and section changes. Treat automatic beat and lyric alignment as candidates and check uncertain positions against the audio. Map chapters to song sections and let the picture carry a motif or change across them. Align major cuts or impacts with selected beats. Use approved lyrics and actual word timestamps when the brief includes on-screen lyrics. Avoid adding sound effects that mask the music accents.
5. For a multi-session film, keep the approved decisions, current export identity, open notes and next action in the existing project spec or state file. Record revision notes with timecode and the change made. Preserve approved cuts when making local fixes. Revisit the affected beats when the requested change alters the story or runtime.

## Review the draft and export

Use the current draft, contact sheet and selected frames to check the opening action, the strongest beat, generic images or language, the weakest stretch, major sound sync points and reading time. Give each confirmed issue a timecode and prioritize the few changes with the largest impact. Mechanical measurements inform the review. Verify the final file using the [export checks](review-and-delivery.md), including sound playback when available.

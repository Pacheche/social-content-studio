# Voice, hooks and evidence bank

Load when writing or fixing copy, captions and comments, especially on LinkedIn, and when adapting a piece to another network. It complements the anti-slop review in [strategy and copy](strategy-and-copy.md). It does not replace it.

## Brand evidence bank

Generic copy appears when the agent has nothing to write with. Before drafting, gather (or ask for once) a short bank that lives in the brand's project, never in the shared skill:

| Material | What it is for |
| --- | --- |
| Real cases and scenes, with date and scope | Examples and openings nobody else can publish |
| Own numbers with unit, period and base | Figures that can be defended in front of a client |
| Turning points, mistakes and corrections | Opinion with a cost. Without this the tone turns neutral |
| Positions the brand defends and those it avoids | Editorial limits and consistent points of view |
| Phrases the audience already uses to describe their problem | The reader's vocabulary instead of the industry's |

Rules: ask for what is missing in one grouped round and save the answer so later pieces do not repeat it. Each item carries provenance and status (confirmed, inferred, to verify). If the bank has no material for the requested angle, say so and propose another angle. Do not fill the gap with invented experiences. Whoever signs the piece answers for every figure: if they would not stand behind it in a meeting, it is removed before the final.

## Hook mechanism catalog

A hook is a mechanism, not a stock phrase. Choose it according to the piece's goal and record which one was used. The names are our own. There are more variants than the ones listed.

| Mechanism | Works when |
| --- | --- |
| Specific, verifiable fact | A number or a scene surprises without adjectives |
| Curiosity gap with a deliverable promise | The body delivers what the first line hints at |
| Before and after with the same variable | An honest comparison exists, with base and period |
| Question the reader already asks | The doubt is real and the content answers it |
| Anaphora or repetition with a twist | The content is a list whose last item changes the meaning |
| Own mistake with an applicable lesson | The evidence bank has a concrete case |
| Thesis with real contrast | There is a defensible position, not a false binary |
| First-person scene of someone else | There is an authorized case with protected data |

Avoid: invented urgency, reveals without content, hooks the body does not deliver and formulas repeated piece after piece. Two hooks with different mechanisms are enough when there is an editorial doubt.

## Learn from a piece that worked

For a reference, someone else's or your own, that performed: identify the hook mechanism, the body structure and the type of closing, and write a template with blanks for your own topic. Copy the structure, not the phrase or the anecdote. If you cannot isolate what worked, say so. Do not attribute performance to a single cause with a small sample.

## Density of AI patterns

The isolated character is no longer the signal. Density matters. When reviewing:

- Count per paragraph the dramatic connectors (dashes, reveal colons), stacked short sentences and adjectives that replace a number. One dash every so many words is a density cap, not a ban on the sign
- A concrete number beats an adjective. One sharp idea beats three vague ones
- Lists of telltale words are hypotheses by language and by brand. None is applied as literal censorship
- Allow an audit mode that only flags problems without rewriting, when the request is to evaluate
- The review does not detect authorship or promise to pass detectors. Its sources document this

Derive the voice from real approved texts, counting absences too (what it never does: exclamation marks, emoji, first person, self-praise, summary-style closings). Record the evidence next to each trait and note contradictions between pieces instead of averaging them.

## Adapt the text to the network

Verify with a dated source before relying on them: total and visible characters before the cut, maximum number of slides, link handling and how the platform treats links. Values observed in 2026-08 (verify they still hold): Instagram cuts at about 125 visible caption characters; Facebook, about 477 on desktop; LinkedIn, about 140 on mobile, and a paragraph break inside that span can cut earlier. So the first LinkedIn paragraph holds the complete idea.

- Reusing a piece on another network means a new hook that survives that network's cut. Do not paste the original text
- An external link in the body can reduce reach on LinkedIn, and comments with links are also reported as penalized. Sources contradict each other, so decide a single destination and placement with the user, and state the uncertainty
- Reference range on LinkedIn: posts of about 900 to 1,300 characters and comments of 200 to 350. These are one author's starting points, not laws

## Comment before posting

On a new account or one with an unvalidated voice, starting with comments on posts by the ideal audience is a low-risk way to test the tone. Each draft waits for human approval. Nothing is posted or answered automatically. A comment contributes a concrete idea, not praise.

## One-week plan

When there is something to plan, a week of posts defines per day: topic, format, hook mechanism, CTA, tentative time and how many audience accounts to comment on. Measure before scaling up. See [measurement](measurement.md).

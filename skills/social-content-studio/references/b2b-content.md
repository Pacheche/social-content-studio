# B2B content, lead magnets and campaign diagnosis

Use for founder-led LinkedIn content, content that supports a B2B offer, or an editorial review based on qualified conversations. Reuse the project's brand profile, facts, audience or ideal customer profile (ICP), and existing content records. A one-off post can work from an approved brief.

## Choose a subject and build an editorial map

Pick a subject where a recurring problem of the intended buyers overlaps with work the brand can prove it does. Use customer language, real objections, approved examples and the author's own posts. Verify authorship before treating reposts as voice samples. To propose topics, start with evidence already available and identify any missing proof.

| Angle | Material to collect |
|---|---|
| Tactical | A map of the work and decisions the reader can learn, with a concrete demonstration |
| Result | A real before and after, what changed and the conditions that support the claim |
| Personal | A specific event the author is willing to share and what they learned |
| Insight | A claim the author stands behind and the observation or source that supports it |

Choose angles based on available material. Set a cadence the author can sustain. Consider a newsletter when there is a regular subject, evidence to share and someone to run it. For profile copy in scope, align the headline, banner and About section with the same audience, offer and evidence.

## Draft the post or resource

- For a post, open on a specific verified event, example or finding. Make the value to the reader clear within the visible opening. Check the actual fold when it matters. Use a photo, screenshot or clip when it demonstrates the claim and is cleared to share. Read the draft aloud against the author's usual phrasing and cut lines that do not advance it.
- For a lead magnet, choose one problem the reader already recognizes and resolve the part you promise. Pick the smallest usable result, such as a worked template, guided check or short audit. Make it useful without a meeting. Link the next real action to the next problem the paid offer solves. Consider whether a direct CTA already serves the same audience with less friction.
- For a requested follow-up draft, use the specific post, reply or resource as context. Use an answerable question or an available next step. Distinguish interest in the resource from interest in buying. Drafting, posting and contacting people require their own scope. Apply the package's existing authorization rules for sending and publishing.

## Diagnose the first weak step

1. Define the actual path, for example post exposure, engagement by the intended audience, qualified conversation and booked call. For an outreach campaign already measured by the user, the relevant path may start with invitations, acceptance and people messaged. Use one comparable cohort and time window. Record counts and explicit denominators, with an attribution rule for conversations and meetings. Missing or unavailable data stays unknown.
2. Check volume and maturity before interpreting rates. Compare with the user's prior results in the same channel and segment. Check whether a high-reach piece actually reached suitable buyers. Saves, sends and visits can support the diagnosis. They are separate from attributable leads.
3. Find the earliest weak stage you can support. Read representative replies alongside the message or offer and verify intent labels. Separate wrong audience, unclear proposition, existing alternatives, timing and soft interest. Identify the uncertain stage when the evidence cannot distinguish causes.
4. Propose one change with an owner, the metric it should affect and a date or volume for reassessment. Log results with the post, cohort and topic so the next Gate A can use the evidence. If examining upstream benchmarks, include the source, date, denominator and campaign scope. Avoid using them as posting rules or universal targets.
